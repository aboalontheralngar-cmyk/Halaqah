# P1.24 — Research basis for Quran memorization and revision courses

Date reviewed: **2026-08-08**

The course feature was designed after reviewing current public Quran-program models rather than treating a “course” as only a label on a student.

## Patterns observed

- **Finite course windows:** current intensive and summer programs commonly run inside defined start/end dates, often around one month.
- **Explicit study days and sessions:** programs publish recurring weekdays or daily time windows, so a course should not assume every calendar day is a study day.
- **Separate learning tracks:** current programs distinguish memorization, review/mastery, and recitation-correction tracks; some institutions offer several tracks side-by-side.
- **Level/volume tracks:** learners are grouped by target volume such as the full Quran, 25/20/15/10/5/3/2 juz, or Juz Amma, rather than forcing one workload on every participant.
- **Daily measurable revision:** intensive revision programs use repeated scheduled recitation, and some programs explicitly alternate recitation days with review/makeup or rest days.
- **Flexible delivery:** current courses may be in-person or remote and may offer more than one daily attendance period.

## Public references reviewed

1. **Saudi Press Agency — جمعية رتّل summer intensive program (5 Jul 2026):** a one-month program with six target-volume tracks including the full Quran, 20, 10, 5 and 3 juz, and Juz Amma.
2. **Saudi Press Agency — Jazan summer Quran circles (2 Jul 2026):** a one-month program with level-sensitive tracks ranging from the full Quran through 25/20/15/10/5/3/2 juz plus Noorani learning.
3. **Saudi Press Agency — جمعية كرام summer program (6 Jul 2026):** separate memorization, review, and recitation-correction tracks.
4. **Saudi Press Agency — Prophet’s Mosque summer Quran course (12 Jun 2026):** bounded course dates, in-person/remote participation, and two daily time periods.
5. **Dubai Islamic Affairs and Charitable Activities Department — complete-Quran review course (2026):** structured Monday–Thursday revision/mastery work.
6. **Dubai Islamic Affairs and Charitable Activities Department — Ramadan recitation program (2026):** one or two juz daily, with five recitation days, a review/makeup day, and a rest day.
7. **Dubai Islamic Affairs and Charitable Activities Department — reinforcement circle:** recurring recitation with continuous review and a fixed weekly reinforcement amount.

## Translation into Halaqah

P1.24 therefore models a Quran course with:

- start and end dates;
- selected study weekdays;
- memorization, revision, or mixed track;
- independent memorization unit/amount;
- independent revision unit/amount;
- participating students;
- planned/active/completed/cancelled lifecycle;
- optional notes;
- automatic use of an active course target in the student's daily memorization/review workflow and daily memorization-point calculation.

The implementation deliberately does **not** invent a universal memorization threshold or force one course structure on all halaqat. The teacher chooses the target and unit because the reviewed programs themselves use different levels, volumes, schedules, and delivery modes.
